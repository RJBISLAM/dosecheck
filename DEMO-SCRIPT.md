# DoseCheck: 2-minute demo

Record the live (GitHub Pages) site so judges see the same thing they can open. Use the fictional "Ammu" example. Never show a real prescription with personal details.

**0:00–0:15 Hook.** Show 3–4 real medicine boxes on a table (Napa Extra, any esomeprazole or NSAID box you have).
"My mother sees three doctors. Each one writes a sensible prescription. None of them sees the other two. In Bangladesh, the same medicine is sold under dozens of names, so how would she know two of these are the same drug?" _(Say "a parent" if this isn't literally true.)_

**0:15–0:35 Load the bag.** Click "See Ammu's example", show the four items with their Prescription 1/2/3 tags, then press Compare.
"Ammu has knee-pain medicine from an orthopaedic doctor, an acid reducer from a medicine specialist, a toothache painkiller from her dentist, and Napa Extra from the pharmacy."

**0:35–1:05 The reveal.** Let the map lines animate, then pause on it.
"Two findings. Amber: Naprosyn Plus has esomeprazole hidden inside it, the same ingredient as Sergel. You can't see that from the name. Violet: naproxen and ketorolac are different ingredients but the same WHO drug family, NSAIDs. Simple name matching would miss both."

**1:05–1:20 Evidence and honesty.** Click a MedEx link, then the WHO ATC link.
"Every fact links to its source. DoseCheck doesn't say 'dangerous'. It gives you the exact question to ask your pharmacist."

**1:20–1:40 Real box.** On a phone, photograph a real box with "Read a box photo", then confirm the suggested match.
"It reads the box right on the phone. The photo is never uploaded."

**1:40–1:55 Bangla.** Switch to বাংলা and press শুনুন.
"For my mother, it explains everything in Bangla, out loud."

**1:55–2:00 Close.** Show "Print / save summary".
"One list. Every doctor. A clearer conversation with the pharmacist. DoseCheck."

Tips: keep it under 2:30. Add captions. Use 1080p screen recording plus a phone clip. Upload as public or unlisted on YouTube and test the link in a logged-out window.
